# Google Business Profile and testimonials: step-by-step

For Kyle. This is a task list, not code. Nothing here is deployed. I'm not a lawyer: the legal points below are things to confirm with your attorney, not legal advice.

Why bother: for searches like "financial coach Seattle", a Business Profile is usually worth more than anything on the website itself, and it's free.

## Part 1: Set up the Business Profile

**Before you start**
- Do this **after the new site is live on `www.khpathways.com`** (see DEPLOY.md), so the profile points at the new site.
- Use your real business name exactly as it appears on the site: **KH Pathways LLC**. Don't add keywords ("KH Pathways | Seattle Financial Coach"). Google can suspend profiles for that.
- Have ready: your Google account, the email `khpathways@gmail.com`, a home or other real address (for verification), and the booking link.

**Steps**
1. Go to **business.google.com** and sign in with the Google account you want to own the profile. Use an account you'll keep long-term.
2. Click **Add your business** and enter the name above.
3. **Business type / location.** You work with clients remotely or by appointment, not from a storefront, so choose the **service-area business** option (no customer-facing location). Google's guidance is that businesses where customers don't visit the address should hide it.
4. **Address.** Google still needs a real address to verify you. A home address is fine for that. You can **hide it from the public listing** so only your service area shows. Do not use a PO Box or a virtual office address; Google generally rejects them. You can check or change this later under **Edit profile → Location → Business location**, with the "Show business address to customers" toggle off.
5. **Service area.** Enter Seattle and any nearby areas you actually serve. Google's help says a service area shouldn't extend much beyond about two hours of driving from where you're based. If you coach remotely across the state or country, the profile is mainly a local-search tool, so keep it to what is true.
6. **Category.** Search the picker for terms like "coach" or "consultant" and choose the closest honest match. **Avoid categories that imply you're a licensed advisor or planner.** Your disclaimer says you are not a registered financial advisor, and the profile should say the same thing. Confirm the category choice with your attorney.
7. **Contact details.** Email `khpathways@gmail.com`, website `https://www.khpathways.com`. Skip a phone number unless you want one public.
8. **Verify.** Google will ask you to verify (typically by video, phone, email, or postcard, depending on what it offers). Follow the prompts exactly. A postcard can take about a week. You can't do much in the profile until you're verified.
9. **Fill in the profile** once verified:
   - **Description** (up to 750 characters). Use only claims that are already on your site. A draft:
     > KH Pathways is a financial confidence coaching practice in Seattle, WA, led by Kyle Huber. I teach clients how their money works and give them the knowledge and tools to manage it themselves. Coaching packages include Finance First Steps (1 month) and Financial Empowerment (3 months), plus bespoke coaching for specific topics. Book a free consultation to talk through where you are today. Coaching is educational and is not financial advice.
   - **Services:** add Finance First Steps, Financial Empowerment, and Bespoke Coaching, using the site's one-line descriptions.
   - **Booking / appointment link:** `https://calendar.app.google/NVaX8nhezWKmJPHC7`.
   - **Photos:** the logo (use `public/img/logo-icon.svg` exported as PNG, or the existing icon) and the cropped portrait. Avoid the unretouched sweatshirt original (Wrapbook logo). Use `public/img/kyle-about.jpg` instead if you want a photo of you.
   - **Hours:** set "by appointment" if offered, or leave unset.
10. **Keep it consistent.** Business name, email, website, and the "Seattle, WA" wording should match the site exactly. Consistency is a trust signal for search engines.

**After setup**
- Check the profile once a month for Google's edits and any questions.
- Add the profile link to your email signature and LinkedIn.
- Don't add fake reviews, pay for reviews, or ask only happy clients for reviews (see below).

## Part 2: Testimonials

You have clients willing to provide testimonials. That's the strongest thing you can add to the site. How to do it cleanly:

**Asking**
1. Ask by email, not in a meeting, so there's a written record. A starter message:
   > Hi [Name], thank you again for working with me. If you're comfortable, I'd be grateful for a short testimonial about your experience coaching with me, in your own words. I'd like to use it on my website [and Google profile]. You're welcome to say no. If yes, please tell me how you'd like your name shown (full name, first name and last initial, or anonymous), and I'll send the exact text back for your approval before I publish anything.
2. **Don't offer anything of value for a review** (discounts, gifts), and **don't suggest what it should say.** The FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews and testimonials bars compensation conditioned on a particular sentiment, and bars fake or misleading reviews. Small businesses are covered.
3. **Don't write it for them.** The words must be the client's own. Light edits for typos are fine if they approve the final text.

**Permission and privacy**
- Get **written approval of the final wording and the name format** (an email reply saying "approved" is fine). Keep the email.
- Clients' finances are private. Make sure the testimonial reveals no account details, income, debts, or anything identifying beyond what they've approved.
- Never publish a testimonial from someone who hasn't answered.

**Content to watch**
- Your disclaimer says you don't promise outcomes. A testimonial that claims specific financial results ("I paid off $40,000 in a year") can read as a result claim and may raise advertising-rule issues. Prefer testimonials about **the experience**: clarity, confidence, feeling understood, learning to manage things themselves. Ask your attorney how to handle any quote that mentions numbers or results.
- If a client is a friend, relative, or employee, say so next to the quote.

**Adding them to the site**
When you have two or three approved quotes, send them to me with the approved name format and I'll add a testimonials section to the home page, in the same style as the rest of the site. I won't write or change a quote.

**Google reviews**
- You can also invite the same clients to leave a Google review once the profile is live (Google gives you a review link in the profile dashboard).
- Ask **every** client, not only the happy ones ("review gating" is against Google's rules and the spirit of the FTC rule).
- Respond to every review politely, positive or negative, without sharing any client details.
